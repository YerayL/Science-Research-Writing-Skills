# Glossary — Science Research Writing

**Achievement** — What the study successfully accomplished internally, linked to resolving the specific research question (Ch 4)

**Active Voice** — Sentence structure where the agent performs the action (We investigated…); preferred for describing your own actions (Ch 1)

**Background Information** — Factual context provided in Present Simple tense to support reader understanding, can appear anywhere in the paper (Ch 1)

**Certainty Continuum** — Spectrum from speculation to proof; guides verb choice and language strength when presenting results and implications (Ch 3)

**Citation Reference** — Attribution to previous work; should be placed at the point in the sentence where it resolves ambiguity about who did what (Ch 8)

**Component (Model Component)** — A functional element of a writing model (e.g. "Establish significance," "Identify a gap") that describes what the text is doing, not what it is saying (Ch 0, 1)

**Contribution** — The outward-facing impact of the study on knowledge, research, or the real world; distinct from internal achievement (Ch 4)

**Evaluative Comment** — Language that tells the reader what data means (e.g. "only 43%" or "as high as 43%"), part of the narrative wrap (Ch 3, 8)

**Function (vs. Content)** — What a sentence or paragraph is doing (e.g. identifying a gap) rather than what it is saying (e.g. "polymer properties") — the basis of transferable writing models (Ch 0, 1)

**Gap** — A missing piece in the existing research that your study addresses; may be explicit or implicit (Ch 1)

**Generic Model** — A template of sentence/paragraph functions derived from reverse-engineering multiple successful papers; serves as a flexible menu, not a rigid prescription (all Units)

**Genre** — The type of text (research article, grant proposal, conference abstract, review article); each genre requires a different structure (Ch 0)

**'Happy' Words** — Positive vocabulary that communicates the value/achievement of the study (e.g. novel, cost-effective, efficient, robust, first, significant) (Ch 1, 3, 6)

**IMRD** — Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion — the traditional structure of a research article, though increasingly flexible in modern publishing (Ch 0)

**Information-Surfing** — Modern reading behaviour where readers scroll titles/abstracts rapidly to find relevant content; shapes how papers must be written (Ch 0, 2, 6)

**Magnetic South** — The destination point toward which everything in the text should lead consistently (Ch 8)

**Modal Verb** — Verbs expressing possibility (may, might, could), expectation (should), necessity (must), or conditionality (would); critical for Discussion precision (Ch 4)

**Narrative Wrap** — The communication scaffold that surrounds information, giving it function and direction for the reader (Ch 0)

**Narrowing/Widening** — The Introduction narrows from general to specific; the Discussion widens from specific results back to broader context (Ch 1, 4)

**Passive Voice** — Sentence structure where the action is emphasised over the agent (Cells were detached…); common in Methods sections (Ch 1, 2)

**Repetition Linkage** — Repeating a key word from the end of one sentence at the start of the next to create cohesion (Ch 1)

**Reverse Engineering** — Active reading strategy of deconstructing successful texts to identify the functions of sentences/paragraphs and build writing models (Ch 0)

**Sentence Startup** — The opening words of a sentence; should help the reader see how this sentence connects to the previous one (Ch 1, 8)

**Structured Abstract** — Abstract with explicit subheadings (Background/Aims, Methods, Results, Conclusions); one of four abstract types (Ch 6)

**Subtitle** — Section heading within the paper; should accurately represent subsection content and contain keywords used early in the subsection (Ch 8)

**Supplementary Materials (SI)** — Additional content placed outside the main manuscript; increasingly used for detailed Methods but with citation and peer-review implications (Ch 2)

**Target Articles** — A set of recently-published, successful papers in your specific field and target journal used as models for reverse engineering (Ch 0)

**Verb Tense as Signal** — The choice between Past Simple (tentative, specific finding) and Present Simple (established truth, permanent knowledge) communicates confidence level (Ch 1, 3)
