# Writing the Title

## Core Idea
The title is the most read part of any paper. In the era of information-surfing, a strong title determines whether your paper is found and whether it is read. A poor title may cause your study to be overlooked entirely.

## Title Quality Checklist (7 Steps)

### 7.1 Check Average Length
- Compare your title length against titles in your target journal
- Length conventions vary by field — some use long descriptive titles, others short declarative ones
- Too short: may not contain enough keywords for searchability
- Too long: may not be fully visible in search results or scroll lists

### 7.2 Using Acronyms
- Only use acronyms that are universally recognised in your field (e.g. DNA, PCR)
- Avoid field-specific acronyms in the title — they reduce searchability for interdisciplinary readers
- If in doubt, spell it out

### 7.3 Compare Title Keywords to the Keyword List
- Ensure your title contains the same keywords you'd use to search for your paper
- Missing keywords = missed by search engines and readers
- Check keywords in successful titles from your target journal

### 7.4 Check the Grammar of the Title
- Titles can be: noun phrases (most common), full sentences, or questions
- Noun phrase titles: "Development of inlaid electrodes for whole column electrochemical detection"
- Sentence titles: less common but used in some fields (e.g. medical journals)
- Question titles: increasingly used to engage readers

### 7.5 Map and Model the Structural Content of Titles in Target Articles
- Reverse-engineer title patterns: What information do they contain? What order?
- Common elements: topic, method, key finding, application, material/system studied
- Build a model of typical title structure for your target journal

### 7.6 Check that Expectations the Title Suggests are Fulfilled in the Paper
- The title is a promise to the reader — every element should be addressed in the paper
- If the title mentions an application, the paper must address it
- Mismatched expectations damage credibility

## Anti-patterns
- **Working title as final title**: The title you used during the project may not effectively communicate the final paper
- **Keyword-stuffing**: Forcing every keyword into the title makes it unreadable
- **Clickbait titles**: Over-promising in the title erodes trust when the paper doesn't deliver
- **Acronym overload**: Reduces discoverability and alienates non-specialist readers

## Key Takeaways
1. The title is a promise — ensure the paper delivers on every element
2. Include the same keywords readers would use to search for your paper
3. Reverse-engineer 10-20 titles from your target journal to build a structural model
4. Check length conventions — too long or too short both hurt visibility
5. Spell out acronyms unless universally recognised
6. Titles have become more important as online scrolling/searching dominates
