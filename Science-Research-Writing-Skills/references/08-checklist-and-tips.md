# Checklist and Tips

## Core Idea
A condensed quality-control checklist that summarises writing issues from the entire book. Use before submission as a final audit.

## Guiding Principles
1. The aim is to make it **impossible for the reader not to understand**
2. Data/information alone have no intrinsic function without a narrative
3. Knowing WHAT to write ≠ knowing HOW to write it
4. Your familiarity with the material makes it harder to see potential ambiguities
5. Good organisation + good writing can compensate for language errors; error-free language does NOT compensate for poor organisation
6. Everything should lead toward the 'magnetic south' — the destination point

## 8.1 Organising the Information

### Planning
- List the study's achievements in order of importance before writing
- Identify specific contributions to (i) knowledge, (ii) research, (iii) the real world
- **Step away from the keyboard** — plan the order of each section before typing sentences
- Effective planning increases both writing speed and quality

### The Value of the Study
- Explicitly and consistently identify achievements using 'happy' words
- Be specific about contribution (e.g. "opening a new direction in research", not "this is important")
- State potential impact even if it seems obvious to you

### Subsections & Subtitles
- Use keywords from the subtitle as early as possible in the subsection
- Lead from those keywords to help the reader enter the subsection

### Ordering Information
- Don't jump from general → specific without bridging
- The space between full stop and next capital letter is dangerous — link logically
- Wrap information in narrative: comment on data (e.g. "only 43%" or "as high as 43%")

### Relevance
- Anticipate the "so what?" question at the end of each sentence
- Resolve with phrases like "suggesting that…", "which means that…"

## 8.2 Creating Sentences

- **Sentence length**: Vary sentence length; very long sentences lose readers
- **Sentence starts**: Avoid starting every sentence with the subject; use connector words, This/These + noun, or repetition linkage
- **Paragraph-entry sentences**: The first sentence of each paragraph should signal its function
- **One idea per sentence** (where possible): Multiple ideas per sentence create ambiguity

## 8.3 Grammar and Vocabulary

- **Consistent terminology**: Don't vary key terms for "elegant variation" — it confuses global readers
- **Verb tense discipline**: Each tense choice communicates something about the status of the information
- **Article usage** (a/an, the, Ø): Particularly challenging for non-native speakers; get it right for key claims
- **Countable/uncountable nouns**: Affect article choice and verb agreement
- **Modifiers**: Place modifiers close to what they modify

## Anti-patterns
- **Starting to type before planning**: Committing to sentences prematurely wastes time
- **Assuming the reader sees what you see**: Your deep familiarity is a blind spot
- **Inconsistent vocabulary**: Using different terms for the same concept confuses readers
- **Missing the 'so what?'**: Every sentence should have clear relevance to the overall narrative

## Key Takeaways
1. Use this checklist as a final pre-submission audit
2. Plan before typing — this is the single highest-leverage writing habit
3. Add evaluative comments to data (naked numbers are meaningless)
4. Anticipate and resolve the "so what?" question throughout
5. Be explicit about achievement, contribution, and impact — do not assume readers will infer them
