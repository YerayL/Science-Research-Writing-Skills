# Appendix B: Research Verbs

## Core Idea
A curated list of verbs commonly used in science research writing, organised functionally. Use this as a reference when drafting to avoid repetition and find the most precise verb for your intended meaning.

## Key Verb Categories

### Causal Verbs
cause, produce, yield, generate, create, drive, give rise to, lead to, result in, result from, arise from, originate in, contribute to, be a factor in, affect, influence, govern, initiate, account for

### Verbs of Investigation
analyse, assess, calculate, calibrate, characterise, collect, compare, compute, conduct, consider, construct, determine, evaluate, examine, explore, identify, investigate, measure, model, monitor, observe, quantify, record, test, verify

### Verbs of Demonstration/Proof
confirm, corroborate, demonstrate, establish, prove, show, validate, verify, substantiate

### Verbs of Reporting/Claiming
argue, claim, conclude, hypothesise, postulate, propose, report, speculate, suggest, appear to, seem to

### Verbs of Agreement/Disagreement
accord with, agree with, compare well with, complement, conflict with, contradict, correspond to, correlate with, be consistent with, be in line with

### Verbs of Change/Development
adapt, adjust, alter, change, convert, decline, develop, enhance, improve, increase, modify, reduce, shift, transform, vary

### Verbs of Achievement
achieve, acquire, address, adopt, apply, assemble, attain, carry out, complete, create, deal with, enable, establish, facilitate, implement, obtain, overcome, perform, permit, provide, resolve, solve, succeed in

### Verbs of Attribution/Classification
ascribe, assign, associate with, attribute to, be confined to, categorise, classify, connect to, couple, link to, relate to

## Anti-patterns
- **Elegant variation**: Don't rotate through synonyms just to avoid repetition — use the most precise verb consistently
- **Overclaiming**: Don't use "prove" when "suggest" or "indicate" is more accurate (see Certainty Continuum in Writing about Results)
- **Underclaiming**: Don't use "may suggest" when your evidence clearly "demonstrates"

## Key Takeaway
Build your own discipline-specific verb list by mining your target articles — this appendix provides the starting framework.
